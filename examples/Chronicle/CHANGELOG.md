# Chronicle changelog

What changed in the game, build by build, as a player sees it. The engine's own changes are in
the `CHANGELOG.md` at the root of the repository.

## 0.8 - unreleased: the stores, the familiars and the town

A life now gathers: piles of berries, water, firewood and stone that grow month by month, small
creatures bound to him that make more of them, and a stash that keeps what he held for the life
after him. The town is built of what he gathers, and gives back what gathering needs: room for
another familiar, a new kind to bind, a bigger stash. And the Collegium is no longer two years
at work: it is a term of sixteen days, each chosen when it comes.

### New

- **Berry pies.** From seven the baker on the square pays 6 coin and a little Dexterity for a
  two-month bake, if he brings 6 berries.
- **Resources.** Berries, water, firewood, stone and clay have their own place in your ledger,
  under RESOURCES, with the most you can hold of each (30 berries, 40 water, 30 firewood, 20 stone,
  20 clay). Gems are made, never gathered, 20 at most. Walks still bring them, and a
  walk's haul is never thrown away, full or not.
- **Scavenging.** From seven a boy can pick berries on the hedge, draw water from the bell and
  gather deadfall, and from eight pick stones from the fields: a month of each, and the pile grows.
  Full, the row waits until you have room.
- **Your stores.** A berry basket, a rain barrel, a roofed woodpile and a stone heap each hold 20
  more of their resource for the rest of the life, three times over. They cost a little firewood
  (the woodpile some stone too), and come once you hold some of what they store.
- **The market trades them.** Sell water, firewood, stone and clay by the lot; once you know the
  town, buy water, firewood and stone.
- **Familiars.** After his first two scavenges a boy can bind a hedge sprite: it eats a ration a
  month and makes 4 berries. A familiar is bound for life, it cannot be sent away, and it ends with
  him. Every month he eats first, then each kind is fed in turn, all of it or none: a hungry kind
  makes nothing that month, and costs him nothing else. The row and its tooltip say what a
  creature eats and makes before you bind it, and your rations warning counts their share, at work
  with meals too: the months it says are the months that feed him and them both.
- **Slots.** A child keeps one familiar. A first class gives a second slot, a second calling two
  more, and the Harrow relic one while he holds it.
- **More kinds.** *Read of the Well Spirits*, once he has his letters, teaches the well imp (8
  water a month, for a ration and a coin); *Learn the Kitchen Charms*, once he has helped in the
  kitchen, the kitchen brownie, which turns berries, water and firewood into rations. Beat the Old
  Shell and the stone mole is yours; beat the Old Grey and the gem weaver, which spins stone into
  gems. *Study the Stone-Weavers' Craft*, from fourteen with Letters 2 and some Lore, teaches the
  gem weaver too, for 5 stone and 5 water: the gems of the town's higher levels wait on no boss.
  A kind once learned stays learned, in every life after.
- **Lessons.** A bound familiar can be taught twice, each lesson costing a month of what it eats:
  every one of that kind makes one more a month from then on.
- **The stash.** At his death, what he held of each basic resource goes into the stash, after the
  gift to the town takes its share, up to 100 of each. A new life takes from it with *Take ... from
  the stash*, as much as it has room for; the rest waits for the lives after. The ending says what
  goes into it, and says it again when you choose a place for the gift.
- **What the town gives back.** Some levels now help the gathering: the market hall makes every
  sprite bring one more berry, the mill loft and the Collegium's reading desk each give every
  life a slot for one more familiar, the smithy's second bellows teaches the stone mole and the
  chapel primer the well imp (no need to beat the Old Shell or read of the well spirits), the
  storyteller's chair at the inn lets the stash keep 50 more of each thing, and the lodge's racks
  make every mole dig one more stone.
- **The Storehouse.** A new place of the town, there once the stash holds anything. What you
  take from the stash is taken there, on the Town page, once the town is known. Its three levels
  let the stash keep 100, 200 and then 400 more of each thing.
- **Gear from the smithy.** Once the smithy has its second bellows it forges an iron blade
  (10 stone, 10 firewood and a gem: 4 more Might) and a leather jerkin (4 hides and 6 firewood:
  2 armour off every bite or blow a beast lands). With the practice blades, and once a marsh
  light has used its cold fire on you, it forges a warded charm too (3 gems and 10 water: 2 off
  every working). Each is forged once a life, held under GEAR, taken into every fight, and lost
  at his death: it is never kept in the stash nor left to a place.
- **Millbrook Marsh.** A new place to walk, from 18, once the Quarry Road is known 4 of 4: three
  months a walk. Marsh lights are workings, not bodies: an edge goes through them as through
  smoke, a blessing ends them, and armour does nothing against their cold fire. Bog hounds hunt
  in threes and frost hurts them more; a leech mass mends at every turn of its own unless it
  burns. Over the deepest pool waits the Lantern Mother, who calls a light to her as she is hurt.
  The marsh gives pitch, and bog iron once it is known 3 of 4; a light sometimes leaves a bead of
  wisp-glass. Beat the Lantern Mother and the marsh wisp is yours to bind: it eats a ration and 2
  water a month, makes a reagent, and is bound with 2 pitch.
- **Options.** A fourth tab, after Adventure. Begin on first click makes a click on a task start
  it at once (off as before: a click chooses, Begin or a double click starts). The light of the
  page is Scriptorium (day) or Candle (night), and the game opens in the one you chose. Export
  writes your save to a file of yours, Load brings one back (the game says so when a file is not a
  save, or is from a newer version, and asks before it replaces a game under way), and Burn the
  chronicle erases every life, the town and all knowledge once you type BURN. Your settings are
  kept in your save, and loading another save or burning the chronicle leaves them as they are; an
  exported file holds the chronicle alone. Not on the phone page yet.
- **The Collegium, day by day.** *Enter the Collegium* opens a term of sixteen days on a page of
  its own, and his two years there pass as the days do: the gauge at the head says the day and
  the months gone, and how the masters think of him is said in words under his Vitality, by what
  he has done so far (they tell him in time when they seldom see him in their lecture halls). Each
  day he chooses its work. In the first weeks every hall is open: the elemental lectures, the
  familiar studies, the library, copying for the scriptorium (it pays), a day's rest. Each course
  is paid at its door, 3 coin a day. A stage's first day writes its words to the record.
- **The choice of an element.** Twice at the elemental lectures by the seventh day, he gives his
  name to fire, frost or storm, or keeps to the basics of all three. Two days on his element and
  its bolt is his (*Firebolt*, *Frostbolt*, *Stormbolt*, the last a shock); four of the basics
  teach *Spark*, *Rime* and a new *Static*. Three days of the familiar studies open the binding
  hall, where he learns how the well imp is bound, or the kitchen brownie once he knows the imp.
  Who has a working to throw can practise on the dummies.
- **The examinations.** The fourteenth day is the last of study. On the fifteenth the masters
  send away who has followed too few courses; the others sit the theory, answered from what was
  taught, from the old tome, from the forbidden shelf (it counts for more, and he is suspected
  of it) or from the Church fathers. What his childhood gave him counts there: his letters, the
  tome, the library, Mara, the shelf, the Church, the old chronicle read. On the sixteenth, the
  practical: two dummies, by his own hand, before the masters call time. He passes, with merit
  or with distinction (an answer of his own, or days on the dummies), or fails. A pass makes him a
  scholar of the Collegium, as the term did. What the term taught him, and that he left it, are
  rows of the Life page.
- **Chance days in the term.** A second-year's notes, fever in the dormitory, a master's errand
  for the top floor: each asks an answer before the day's work.
- **Who leaves keeps what he learned.** *Leave* is asked twice. Who leaves, is sent away or fails
  has dropped out: what the term gave him is his, a course's bolt with it, the months not lived
  there are his again, and its rations for them go back. The Collegium does not take him back,
  but the other classes stay open: a man of the Keep who had followed the fire course throws a
  Firebolt between two cuts. His chronicle says he left.

### Changed

- **Drying berries reads Make.** Its buttons say Make and Make all, as the market's making does.
- **A level of the town is paid mainly in resources.** The first level of a place asks 20 each of
  the two basics it is built of; the second 40 of them and 2 gems; the third 80, 4 gems and one
  thing found on a walk (pelts, hides, fangs, a white antler, bog iron, wisp-glass, or a boss's
  trophy). Clay is never asked past 20, since no store makes room for more, and the storehouse
  asks firewood too from its second level. Their coin is about a quarter of what it was. Some
  levels still ask a part or a standing as well, and the lodge is still built of what walks
  bring.
- **What a death leaves to a place** is anything its next level still asks, not only what walks
  bring: berries, water, firewood, stone, clay, gems and things found. Never a part, a skill or a
  standing: renown is not left to anyone. When a level is raised, what is left over in its fund
  stays for the level after.
- **The mill loft** gives a familiar slot, no longer 6 rations at birth; **the reading desk** a
  familiar slot, no longer Lore 1.
- **Early works ask for resources.** The kitchen wants water and firewood, the smithy firewood,
  your letters a little firewood, and a remedy a cup of water. The carters still take anyone, so a
  boy with nothing can always eat.
- **Berries, firewood, stone and clay are resources now**, no longer stores, each with its limit.
  What an old save holds past a limit stays: it is only spent, never cut.
- **Dry berries at home.** Drying berries into rations is among what a boy gathers, no longer at
  the market: a sprite's berries feed him before he knows the town.
- **Dry berries asks only for its berries.** Its tooltip has one line, the three berries it
  costs, no longer a second "Berries to dry"; its row comes with his first berries.
- **No bind rows without a free slot.** While every slot is filled the *Bind* rows leave the
  list (the lessons stay). A slot from a class, a relic or the town brings them back, with no
  "New:" toast for a row he has seen before.
- **Work that fed him now gives its months of rations when he begins it**: three for the
  carters, eighteen for the Keep's oath, kept past the limit of sixty. He and his familiars eat
  every month at any work, so rations go one way only. The tile says RATIONS UP FRONT, the
  tooltip "Rations 3 up front", and the log what was given. Left before its term (set aside for
  an answer, and closed before he could go back), the rations of the months not worked go back.
  A save made in the middle of such a work is given the rations of the months it has left, once,
  as it opens. The gleaning of his sixth year no longer counts as feeding him: a boy who holds no
  rations eats none, and what it brings is his first.
- **The rations guide** no longer says "3 months": with familiars bound, three rations last less.
- **The market buys and sells on lines of their own.** Under *Buy* and *Sell* (what is made there
  under *Make*), and its button says *Buy*, *Sell* or *Make*, not *Do it now*. Beside it, *Sell all*
  sells every lot you hold, *Buy all* buys as many lots as your coin and your room allow
  (rations, reagents, water, firewood, stone; not what is bought once), and *Make all* makes
  as many as you can pay for and have room for (remedies, fire flasks). The *All* button now
  stands beside its button, where it was meant to be.
- **The Town page** no longer writes "The Market" over its lines: *Buy*, *Sell*, *Make* and each
  place have their own heading.
- **A place's tile** says a short line for what its level gives, so the end is no longer cut off;
  its tooltip still says all of it. The second bellows say the smithy forges gear now, and its
  practice blades that it forges the warded charm.
- **Start Helping Out** gives its 6 rations as he begins it, no longer 1 then 6 at its term: the
  guided first year no longer says his rations are running out the month before he is seven.
- **A fed work's tooltip says how long its rations last** when his familiars eat them sooner than
  its term: "Rations 24 up front · lasts 6 mo".
- **The Collegium takes no coin up front.** Its door asks Intelligence 12, Letters 3 and Lore 2,
  and coin for its first days (6, held, not taken); the fees of its courses are paid as he
  follows them: about 35 coin for a whole curriculum, as before, but a day at a time.
- **Firebolt is taught by the fire course**, no longer by the Collegium itself. A save that holds
  the Collegium keeps its Firebolt; one made in the middle of the old two years is given what the
  term gave, as it opens, and its months run out as they were.

### Fixed

- **His age** at the head of the page could read a year short in the month of his birthday
  (15 at sixteen, until the next month).
- **No more immortal old men.** An Inn raised after his thirtieth year stopped his old age on the
  spot: the Vitality it had taken stayed lost, the works of his prime came back, and he lived
  untouched to fifty. Where his old age begins is now settled the month he turns thirty: an Inn
  raised later is for the lives after his, and its first level says so (old age at 50, not 30).

## 0.7 - unreleased: the fight

The walks become the heart of a life: a fight is now about how you fight, and what you learned
growing up is what you bring.

### New

- **Who is quicker acts more often.** A fight is no longer rounds. The order of the turn shows
  the next six actions, yours first; a quick move (a thrust, a step aside) brings your next
  action sooner, a heavy one (a finisher) later.
- **Kinds of blows, and beasts that resist them.** A blow cuts, pierces or crushes; a working
  burns, freezes, shocks, or is holy. A boar's hide turns an edge but not a hammer; a beetle's
  plates stop a cut but not a point. Armour stops blows (a pierce goes through), a ward stops
  workings. Once you know a beast well, every move says *WEAK* or *RESISTED* against it, and a
  beast's tooltip says what it is weak to and what it resists. Lore knows a beast a level better,
  and finds its weakness deeper.
- **What is on a beast.** Burning hurts it at its turn, chilled slows it, shocked makes every hit
  bite harder, exposed means your next hit is a sure crit. A big beast has poise: wear it down
  (a hammer twice as fast) and it staggers, its next turn far off and its guard open.
- **Four ways to fight.** *The Keep*: every blow that lands builds momentum, and at three a
  finisher (*Heavy cut*, *Sweep*) spends it and goes through half of what the beast resists;
  defending costs a point, *Brace* keeps it. *The Collegium*: one element leaves its mark, a
  second sets it off (fire and frost *Shatter*, fire and storm *Flare* on all of them, frost and
  storm *Freeze*). *The Hidden Hand*: each hit before the beast's turn bites harder and crits more.
  *The Greenwood*: traps set on the walk, and a *Bow shot* that costs no time as your first action.
- **A boy's first tools.** *Learn to Temper* teaches a *Hammer blow*, the yard's bow a *Bow shot*,
  its breathing a *Second wind*, the rooftops *Step aside*, the Boys' oath a *Dirty kick* and
  turning them in *Brace*, your letters *Read the beast*, the forbidden shelf *Spark*, the
  sacristan's keys a *Blessing*, Mara a *Rime*. Following the hunters finds beasts a day sooner
  and skins them better. Everything you have learned is yours in every fight.
- **How to meet it.** Who can hide, has set a trap or holds a ration for a pack chooses how to
  meet a beast found: lie in wait, lead it into the snare, draw one off, or leave it be.
- **A fire flask and blessed oil**, from the market: a flask burns any beast the same, past its
  hide; oil makes your blows holy for the fight.
- **New beasts.** A rat nest and the Old Pike at Mill Pond, shell beetles and the Old Shell on the
  Quarry Road, and the Old Grey, who leads his wolves and calls the wood when hurt. The White Stag
  bolts once hurt.
- **Bosses.** The Old Pike, the Old Shell and the Old Grey are met by choice, from a place known
  4 of 4, and beaten once a life, for a trophy and a page of the town's story. Fall to one and the
  life ends there.

### Changed

- **A kill trains nothing in you.** The first kill of each kind of beast in a life is a lesson
  about it (it is known 3 of 4 from then on), and beasts leave a little more. A technique used
  often rises a rank: a little stronger, and a point of what it trains.
- **Brought down, you lose the walk, not your health.** Carried home from an ordinary fight, you
  lose what the walk had gathered, and no longer any of your most Vitality.
- **Blows aim with Dexterity**, workings with Intelligence; a working is no longer stopped by
  armour, and Arcana makes it stronger.

## Unreleased

After comments on 0.6.1: a town that spent on one click, a tooltip stuck over the page, and
hazelnuts sold one press at a time.

### Changed

- **The town works as the tasks do.** A click on a place of the town chooses its next work, as a
  click on a task does: its tooltip and your parts say what it asks, and the button under *At
  work now* begins it. A double click still starts it at once, on a place as on a task.
- **Do it now while at work.** Something done at once (a sale, a buy) or an answer to an event
  gets its button under your running work, months stopped or not.
- **The button stays put.** What you did at once stays chosen for the next press. Once there is
  none left, its button stays where it was, greyed (*None left*), until you click anywhere else:
  *Pass a month* no longer comes up under a mouse still clicking.

### New

- **All.** Beside *Do it now*, for what you sell or make from what you hold (hides, berries,
  hazelnuts, a remedy, rations): sells or makes as much as you can in one press, with one line in
  the log.
- **Tooltips on a touch screen.** Once a finger touches the screen, a tap on a task shows what it
  asks, whatever the size of the screen, and it stays until the next tap.

### Fixed

- A tooltip could stay frozen over the page (one was seen for *Swear Service to the Keep*),
  covering the tabs and buttons under it.

## 0.6.1 - 2026-10-17

A hotfix for the events of a life (the Flood, the Levy, the Plague Year and the Old Lord's
death), after the first comments on 0.6: missing two of them could leave twenty years with
nothing to do, and a long task could run over the whole of one before you saw it.

### Changed

- **Standing by costs less.** An event you leave unanswered still closes what it names (the
  Watch, the sacristan's keys and the Bellmoor Boys after the Flood, and so on), and that is all
  it costs: however many you stand by, no class and no second calling is shut by it any more.
- **More time to answer.** The Flood gives you the whole year you are eleven, and the Plague
  Year and the Old Lord's death nine months each to begin an answer, where each gave six. The
  Levy is called three months sooner, at fourteen and nine months, and still settled when the
  classes open. *Take the Sacristan's Keys* and *Read the Forbidden Shelf* open at twelve, once
  the Flood is behind you.

### New

- **An event stops the months.** The month an event comes, the months stop and the log says so;
  if you have not answered, they stop once more on the last month you can. *Go on* (or Space)
  goes on.
- **Events on top.** While an event is open its answers stand at the top of your tasks, framed
  in red, with a red **!** on their corner.
- **Answer now.** With the months stopped, choose an answer and the button under your work
  reads *Answer now*: the work is set aside where it stands, the answer is given, and the work
  goes on from the month it was at. Before, a task of a year or two could not be left, and an
  event that came during it went by.

## 0.6 - 2026-10-16

### New

- **His sixth year.** Every life now begins at 6, and its first year is a row of short tasks, a
  month or two each, that decide who the boy is at seven. You glean for your first rations and
  carry flour for your first coin. Then a first choice, **one of two**: *Climb the Mill Wheel*
  (Strength and Dexterity) or *Sit with the Old Scribe* (Intelligence and Vitality); the one
  you leave is gone. Then **two of three** tasks of two months that close before you can do
  them all: *Learn Knots from the Bargemen* (Dexterity and Intelligence), *Haul Sacks at the
  Mill* (Strength, at a cost in Vitality) or *Sing at the Chapel Door* (Intelligence and
  Renown). Then the market, the harvest (a point of everything), and a month at the mill that
  ends the day you are seven. A whole year takes about a quarter of a minute, and no two need
  be alike.
- **Written time.** While the game is closed the chronicle goes on being written: every
  second away is kept, up to an hour (3600 seconds), and waits in your Inventory beside your
  coin. So is time in the game with nothing running and nobody at it: a minute with no key,
  click or mouse movement while the months are stopped, and from then on it counts. While you have some, the button *Months ×1* in *At work now* spends it: press it for
  ×2, again for ×3, again to stop. The months run that fast until it is used up, then go back to their own pace by
  themselves. It is yours, not his: a new life keeps it.
- **A keepsake.** *Carry Books for Father Anselm* pays in coin and in a small tome he tells you
  to keep. It is in your Inventory. Nothing asks for it yet.
- **The wooden blade is bought, then learned, then chosen.** From two months past seven the
  market sells a wooden blade, once, for 8 coin. Holding one brings *Practice with the Wooden Blade*
  into your training: two months, a point of Arms and the basics of the blade. Then **one of
  two**, three months each: *Practice Slashes* (Strength and Arms) or *Practice Thrusts*
  (Dexterity and Arms); the one you leave is gone. No age closes any of it.
- **Two things to do in a fight, for a boy.** The edge teaches the **Slash** (it bites deeper
  than a strike and misses more), the point the **Thrust** (it seldom misses, and bites a
  little less). They are a better way to strike and cost nothing: no breath, no turn to wait.
  They are yours for the rest of the life, whatever class you take.
- **Watch the Tourney at the Yard.** A month, from nine, for a boy with no blade. It leaves
  you a **Yard enthusiasm**, in your Inventory: with it the sergeants let you train at the
  yard, and your first term there takes it.
- **The yard teaches the basics.** A first term of *Train at the Yard* gives the basics of the
  blade to who came without them, so the edge or the point is theirs to choose too.
- **Learn the Yard's Breathing.** Three months, once, for who has both practiced slashes or
  thrusts and trained at the yard: a point of Discipline and the breathing itself, which
  you keep. Nothing in a fight uses it yet.

- **A childhood of short tasks, 8 to 13.** Eleven new ones, none longer than three months.
  At the smithy, a month watching the master opens *Learn to Temper* (Arms, and the Keep asks
  you for less) or you *Drive for the Carters* (coin, and every walk is a day shorter each
  way): one or the other. At the chapel, *Take the Sacristan's Keys* (Discipline, and the
  Collegium asks less Intelligence) or *Read the Forbidden Shelf* (Lore, and the Harrow
  fragments ask less): one or the other; and *Learn the Herbal*. In the streets, *Snare
  Rabbits on the Common* before eleven, the tunnel under the wall for who has roamed the town
  twice, the smuggler who works through it, and the Boys, who ask for an oath or are turned
  in: the oath shuts the Watch to you, turning them in shuts the Hidden Hand.
- **Things a boy learns that a walk uses.** A snare is a day for two rations; the herbal
  brews a remedy from two wortleaf where you stand; who holds the sacristan's keys rests a
  little better; who knows the roads arrives a day sooner. In a fight, the smuggler's *Sand
  in the eyes* costs a beast its next move.
- **The Flood, at eleven.** In every life, the month you are eleven: six months to answer it,
  a month each, and one answer. Haul sandbags (Strength, and the mill's wheel costs half to
  mend this life), pull folk from the water (Renown, at a cost in Vitality), or salvage the
  wreck (coin and Guile, and Father Anselm will not give you his keys).
- **The Lord's Levy, at fifteen.** Six months to answer, one answer. March for six months (Arms,
  Discipline, the heavy cut, and the Keep asks less Arms), pay the scutage, or hide in the
  Greenwood: a deserter is shut out of the Keep, and the Greenwood is a class he may choose at
  sixteen like the others.
- **A second calling, and twenty more years.** In a town whose Inn stands (its first level),
  a man of thirty is not old: your prime runs to fifty, old age begins there, and the works no
  age closes stay yours. At twenty-nine each class is offered two callings, one or the other:
  the Keep's man becomes the **Defender of Bellmoor** or a **Crusader**, the scholar a
  **Curse-breaker** or a **Spellwright**, the thief an **Assassin** or an **Adventurer**. Each
  is a year to enter and five works after it: its bread, its choice, its moment, and a work of
  two years from forty-two. The Greenwood has none yet. A Spellwright's bound elements are
  spells in a fight (Frostbind, the second firebolt, chain lightning); an Adventurer finds any
  beast two days sooner, and with porters every gathering is half again as much.
- **Every class has its fork, its chain and its deadline.** The Keep: the ambush tracks that
  lead to the Greenwood camp, and a duel for the captain's favour. The Collegium: orders in the
  Church or the demon scholar, one or the other, each with its own account of the curse; the
  margin glyphs that open a cellar under the mill (the mill's loft must stand); the Magister's
  examination. The Hidden Hand: its mark (every favor is one more, silence is free) or your
  own name (the jobs pay more, and earn no favor); the Collegium's stores to steal from; Old
  Vey's last summons. The Greenwood: spare the village carts or take the toll from all; the
  burners' shrine and the woodfolk; the Lord's pardon, which opens the Keep's service and
  closes the Greenwood's own.
- **The Plague Year, at twenty-three, and the Old Lord's death, at twenty-seven.** Six months
  to answer each, one answer. Three answers to the succession ask what only a class gives; the
  fourth, *Kneel to the Heir*, asks nothing and brings nothing.
- **An event left unanswered is paid for.** The Flood, the Levy, the Plague Year and the
  succession each have an answer that asks nothing of you, so standing by is a choice, and the
  worst one. Stand by at one and what it names is closed to you for this life: the Watch, the
  sacristan's keys and the Bellmoor Boys after the Flood; Sergeant Edric, Mara and the night
  market after the Levy; the raid, the Magister's examination, Vey's summons and the Lord's
  pardon after the plague; the tales, the pupils and the moment of your calling after the
  succession. Stand by at two and the next great step is shut: every class before you have
  one (the Greenwood takes who is left, at sixteen), your second calling after. Each answer
  says so in red before you choose, the log writes it the month it happens, and it is in your
  Inventory from then on.
- **A lead.** *Hear the Bargemen's Tale*, a month and 2 coin at nine: Mill Pond is yours to
  walk a year sooner.
- **One or the other is said, in red.** A task that closes another when you take it (the mill
  wheel or the old scribe, slashes or thrusts) says so in its description before you choose,
  and the log writes which one went when its term is done.

### Changed

- **Every life starts smaller.** 6 years old, 4 of each stat and 35 Vitality, with nothing in
  your Inventory, where a life began at 7 with 6 of each, 40 Vitality and a year of rations.
  The sixth year gives it back, your way: you are seven with 5 to 8 in a stat.
- **A child's months go by faster.** A month lasts a second in your sixth year and a second
  and a half until you are ten, then the two seconds it always took. Every life, not only the
  first.
- **Practice with Wooden Blades is gone.** Six months, three times over, for Arms and
  Dexterity: the three short steps above stand in its place. They bring 2 Arms in five months
  where it brought 3 in eighteen.
- **Train at the Yard has to be earned.** Its row comes once you hold an enthusiasm for the
  yard or the basics of the blade, either one, and no longer for Strength alone.
- **The Yard's racks lend the blade.** *Stock the Yard with Blades* made the old practice
  last five terms. It now lends every boy a wooden blade: none has to be bought.
- **Go to the Market replaces Explore Bellmoor.** One month of your sixth year opens the Town
  page, where your coin buys the rations you are short of by then. Every life goes there once:
  what was built in town stays built from life to life, but the page opens the month you go.
- **The tutorial is the sixth year.** A first life is shown its tasks one at a time and the
  guide says a sentence for each: your food, your coin, the choice (both tiles lit), your stats,
  the stopwatch on tasks that close, the market and how to buy, the task that takes two months,
  your age, and the classes the day you are seven. Its sentences are shorter-lived, and none
  asks you to wait for a task of three months any more. Later lives have no guide, and are
  shown the first tasks of the year in the same order: the gleaning, the flour, the choice of
  two, then the books.
- **The work of a class comes in an order.** The day you are of a class you see its first
  work and the great step of your years, locked; each of the others comes once the one before
  is behind you (done, or closed to you: nothing waits on what you missed). The Keep: service,
  the patrols, the tourney, then the Master-at-Arms and the duel, the merchant road after the
  duel. The Collegium: study, then the workings and the Black Treatise, then the night offices
  and Magister Orin, the Harrow fragments after the treatise, the lecture and the apothecary
  after the fragments. The Hidden Hand: its errands, then the informants and the hidden ways,
  Old Vey and the mark or your own name after the informants, the gate and the clerk after the
  hidden ways. The Greenwood: the outlaws once you have poached, the road once you have trained
  with them. No work opens more than two others.
- **Your second calling shows from the day you take a class**, where the Inn stands: its two
  ways, locked, with what they ask, as a child sees the classes from seven.
- **The work you never took as a child is cleared away.** *Help at the Mill*, *Run Messages*
  and *Help in the Kitchen* leave the list at ten if you have never done them, *Help at the
  Market* at eleven, *Work the Smithy* at thirteen. What you did once stays yours, and *Carry
  for the Carters* is always there. *Watch the Master Temper a Blade* closes at twelve and
  nine months, when the tempering it leads to can no longer follow it.
- **A child's work comes over the year, not all on a birthday.** At seven the carters, the
  mill and your letters, with the three classes ahead of you; *Run Messages* a few months later,
  *Help in the Kitchen* later still. At nine the smithy, then the tourney at the yard, the
  bargemen's tale, the chapel and the choir, a couple of months apart; at ten the Watch, then the
  hunters, the Old Chronicle, the Boys and the tunnel; the same at eleven, thirteen and fifteen,
  and in old age. Each still closes when it did. In the Assassin's calling the three contracts
  come four months apart.
- **Two things to buy at the market that each open a task.** A *snare wire* (2 coin, from nine
  and eight months) before *Snare Rabbits on the Common*, and a *herbal primer* (4 coin, from ten
  and a half) before *Learn the Herbal*. Both are kept in your Inventory. *Run with the Bellmoor
  Boys* now waits until you have roamed the town once, or watched its cutpurses.
- **A first step toward each class at seven.** The day you are seven the three classes stand
  locked ahead of you, and within that year each has a short task that gives what it asks: the
  wooden blade for the Keep (its practice gives Arms), your letters for the Collegium, and
  *Watch the Cutpurses at the Fair* for the Hidden Hand, a month from seven and a half, for a
  point of Stealth and a way to the Boys.
- **The places to walk to are tiles**, like the places of the town: its mark and its name, a
  seal for each level you know it to, and a line with what kind of walk it is and what it
  costs, or what it asks. A closed place can be chosen too: the button says what it asks.
- **The Adventure tab says when it has something new.** The small count at its corner used to
  come only when a place opened to you. It now comes with the tab itself, the day of your first
  walk, and whenever a new place appears on the page, open or not yet.
- *Start Helping Out* is the last month before seven now, and brings 6 rations.
- The anonymous play statistics now say which build they are from and which tasks of the sixth
  year were done (which of the two, which two of the three).
- **The task tiles are drawn anew.** Each is framed, its mark before its name, and under them
  its months at the left and at the right the months left to begin it under a stopwatch (in
  red, and its frame with it, when little time is left). One you cannot take yet
  stands on a darker ground, and the one you picked is tinted, with a blue edge.

### Fixed

- **The low-food hint of the tutorial could go unsaid.** It was lost when your rations had run
  low once before the hint was allowed to speak.

## 0.5 - 2026-10-11

### New

- **Walks outside the town.** The Hedgerows are the first of four places to walk to (the
  Hedgerows, Mill Pond, the Hollow Woods, the Quarry Road). The Hedgerows have a tile from the
  start, right under your work: tiles that take you out of the town have a look of their own, a
  red frame and their mark. When you begin it you are taken there: the stay is a number of
  days, and you spend them one choice at a time (gather, study the place, rest, seek a beast or
  watch it, go home). A work is a tile with its name and its days; hover it to read what it is,
  what it costs and what it gives. Mill Pond shows once you have walked the Hedgerows.
- **Fights.** A beast you seek is fought turn by turn. Each beast has a small panel of its own
  across the top, the size of yours, with you and your turn under it: its name, how it stands,
  and the move it is about to make, by its name alone (*Bite*). Hover the move to read what it
  is; what it does to you is written there once it has been used on you, or once you have
  watched the beast from cover. Your turn is a few buttons: strike, defend, take a remedy if you
  hold one, flee. A press does it, and hovering a button says what it would do. A small bar
  fills before each blow, yours and the beast's, so you see it coming; whoever is hurt has their
  vitality bar flash and fall, and what was taken lifts off it; a blow that misses lifts a
  small *MISS* instead. The order of the turn has a panel of its own at the top right: who acts
  when, and how many blows are coming at you. Every three turns of a fight cost a day of the
  stay. You can also let him fight on by himself. Wolves hunt in packs of
  three; one of them hangs back while two stand. No rat is quicker than a boy.
- **What you learn in a life, you fight with.** A man sworn to the Keep has a *Heavy cut*, and
  a veteran of the campaign a *Sweep*. A scholar of the Collegium throws a *Firebolt*, stronger
  with Intelligence, and Magister Orin's assistant knows a *Binding word* that costs a beast its
  move. The streets teach a *Feint*. Nobody else sees these in a fight.
- **The record, and walking it again.** Everything you do on a walk is written down. When you
  are home the record is kept, five to a place, and it can be walked again for you, once or
  several times in a row: no choice to make, no fight to play, and what the beasts drop is
  rolled again each time.
- **The Adventure tab** comes with your first walk, and the walks leave the Life page for it.
  Tabs that are not there leave no gap, and once you know the town they read Life, Town,
  Adventure. A small count at the tab's corner says a new place has opened.
  - *Where to walk* lists every place you know of, the open ones first: what kind of walk it
    is, its months and days, what it gives, how well you know it, and for a closed one what it
    asks and what you are short of. *Set out for the Hedgerows · 1 mo* starts a new walk to the
    place chosen.
  - *Records* is a table of the walks you have kept of that place, the best first: when it was
    walked and in how many steps, what it costs in months and Vitality, what it brings, and a
    *Walk* button that walks it once.
  - *Walk it again* is filled by the record chosen: how many times, when to stop (never, when
    the bag is short, when you hold enough of something, at an age), and four figures for what
    the walks take, use, bring and teach, with *Walk once* and *Queue*. While a queue runs it
    gives way to *At work now*: which walk of how many, and a way to stop after this one.
- **Knowledge.** Studying a place and watching a beast are kept by the world, from life to life:
  beasts get cheaper to find, you see their vitality and what they will do next, then their
  weakness, and a place you know well lets you stay longer.
- **Things to sell, and things to build with.** Berries, wortleaf, fish, reeds, pelts, hides,
  fangs are sold at the market. Wortleaf can be brewed into remedies. Firewood, clay, ash wood
  and stone are what places of the town are built of. All of it is in your Inventory, and all of
  it is lost when the life ends, except what the place you leave your coin to still needs: that
  much stays with the place, and the next life finds it asking less.
- **The Hunters' Lodge.** A tenth place in town, shown once you have walked the Hollow Woods and
  built of what your walks brought back. It opens the Hollow Woods to every later life.
- **Events on the way.** Now and then something waits for an answer: a nest in the hedge, a
  hunter by a cold fire. What you choose is written in the record.
- **Two new tasks.** *Race the Rooftops* (Dexterity and Renown, on the streets, from 11) for
  who means to live by quick hands, and *Keep the Night Offices* (Discipline) at the Collegium.

### Changed

- **A task's time at a glance.** A tile shows how long the task takes in a small box at its top
  right corner. Under its name, at the right, a stopwatch counts the months you still have to
  begin it, for a task that closes: all along for one with a door of its own, and in its last
  two years for the rest. It turns red in the last six months.
- **Sharp on a dense screen.** In a browser on a 4K monitor or a phone the game was drawn small
  and stretched. It is drawn at the screen's own resolution now.
- **The market is in town.** What is sold, bought and brewed at the market is on the Town page
  only: it no longer shows on the Life page before the town is known, and the *Go to the Market*
  tile is gone (the Town tab goes there).
- **Shorter tooltips.** A task no longer lists "Room for Rations" among what it asks while the
  store has room.
- **One Vitality.** Your Vitality is the same number at home, on a walk and in a fight: a boy
  has 40, where he had 8. A month with nothing to eat takes 5, and
  5 come back every two months (every month with a room at the inn). Work that builds Vitality
  brings 5, old age takes 5 every two months, and being carried home from a walk costs 5 of the
  most it can be. A save from before is brought to the new count when it is opened.
- The anonymous play statistics now say more of the walks and of the end of a life: how often
  each place was walked, by hand and by a record, the fights met and how they ended (won, lost,
  fled, left to fight on by himself), the walks he was carried home from, the longest queue, and
  which works of a class and of old age were done. Counts, as before: nothing of who plays.
- **The page is no longer built again for nothing.** When a task ends, only the tiles that
  changed are made again: the others stay where they are, and a tile you had chosen stays
  chosen. When the window changes size, what is on the page takes its new width where it
  stands (on the phone page, on a walk and in a fight too) instead of the whole page being
  built again. It is only built again when the layout itself changes: three columns to one.

### Fixed

- **Resizing the window left a mess.** Dragging the window by its edge, on the page or on a
  walk, could leave pieces of the page drawn over each other at the top left, and they stayed.
  Nothing is left behind now, at any size.

## 0.4 - 2026-10-10

### New

- **The town.** *Explore Bellmoor* (three months, once) opens a Town page beside the Life page.
  Its tile stands out in gold so you do not miss it, and the Town tab only appears once you have
  explored. A short tutorial then shows the tab, the market where your coin buys rations, and
  the places.
  It has nine places of three levels each, and it grows with what you do. At first you see
  two: the Market and the Mill. The others are found by living:
  - the Smithy, once you have worked a term at the forge;
  - the Yard, once you have joined the Border Campaign, and the Watch Gate, once you have
    taken the Captain's Chain;
  - the Chapel, once you have entered the Collegium, the Collegium Gate after assisting
    Magister Orin, and the Harrow Road after the expedition to Harrow;
  - the Inn, from the second life of a world.

  The day a place comes, the work that builds it comes with it. A place you have seen stays
  on the page for your next lives.
- **Works for the town.** On the Town page, under the places, a work raises a place by one
  level. Clicking a place picks its work for you. They cost months and coin, and some cost a
  stat or years of your life. This is time you do not spend on yourself.
- **The town stays.** What you raise is kept when a life ends. The next life is born into that
  town: tasks that are shorter or pay more, classes that ask a little less, and a few things you
  start with (rations, a skill point, a friend at the Watch). The town never gives a class for
  free.
- **The gift at death.** When a life ends with coin, you can leave it to one place. It counts
  toward that place's next level.
- **A word at the end.** When a life ends of old age, one small line under its ending asks for a
  comment or a rating on the game's page. A life cut short is not asked anything.
- **Hover a place** to see what it gives, what its next level asks compared with what you have,
  and who raised it.
- **A notice on a tab.** When something new comes onto the page you are not looking at (a new
  task, something new at the market, a new place), its tab shows a small red count until you
  open it. Only what was never on the page counts: a task you can afford again is not news.
- **The tiles that turn a life stand out.** The four classes and *Explore Bellmoor* have a gold
  frame and their mark in the corner, locked or not.
- **They say what they do.** Hover one of them: it tells you what it changes, with a word from
  the town.

### Changed

- **Buying is done in town.** *Buy rations* and *Buy reagents* are on the Town page, at the
  market. The Life page keeps a *Go to the Market* tile that opens it.
- **Quieter tiles.** A locked tile no longer writes what it needs under its name (an age, a
  stat, coin, room). Its tooltip still says all of it. A tile only says when it is about to
  close.

### Fixed

- Parts of the Town page were sometimes drawn over the Life page, and the other way round, when
  a tile changed on the page you were not looking at.
- A task begun by a double click could not be clicked again until the mouse moved.
- The tutorial could speak of the market in a new chronicle before the town was explored. It
  now comes the first time you open the Town page, on *Buy rations*.

## 0.2 - 2026-10-08

Everything since 0.1, the first build published on itch.io (2026-10-07).

### New

- **A guided start.** A new life opens with its first work already chosen: press Begin and the
  months pass. A line in "At work now" says what to do, and "Pass a month" only comes once a
  first work is done.
- **A word of welcome.** A first life is told in one line what the game is.
- **Classes.** Paths are now called classes. The three of them (the Keep, the Collegium and the
  Hidden Hand) stand in the list from the first day, locked, so you can see what a childhood
  leads to.
- **A fourth class: the Renegade.** Miss all three, and the month the last one closes "Take to
  the Greenwood" opens. It has its own works, a proving, a mastery (King of the Greenwood), two
  deeds and its own ending.
- **A Begin button.** Choosing an activity puts a Begin button in "At work now", with the months
  it takes ("Do it now" for what takes no time). A second click or Enter still begins it.
- **See before you choose.** Pointing at an activity shows what it would bring to your stats
  and to what you hold.
- **Gains you can see.** A figure that rises lifts a "+2" beside it, and the line just written
  in the log is lit for a moment.
- **Toasts.** Deeds, milestones and activities that have just become possible are said at the
  foot of the window.
- **Warnings before it hurts.** Rations running low are said from three months left, with what
  to do about it, and a stat the coming month would take from turns red before the month, not
  after.
- **Locked activities say why.** A locked tile names the first thing it still asks.
- **Limits in sight.** What has a limit reads "12/60" in its row.
- **Smaller windows.** The three columns fit windows down to 680 x 566 (1136 x 664 before), and
  the left column scrolls when it is too tall.
- **Plainer names.** The panels are Inventory, Stats and Log.

### Fixed

- A crash ("Segmentation fault") some players met.
- The Begin button always read "3 mo", whatever was chosen.
- The log lost its last lines when a work began.
- The "+1" of a gain stood a line too high when the same month added a row.
- The page could be drawn half placed for a few frames, and new tiles once without their
  ground. A month is also quicker to work out.
